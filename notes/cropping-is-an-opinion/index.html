Cropping is an opinion

14.09.20264 min read

There's a moment near the end of most projects where an image gets cropped to fit a box. It usually happens fast, often by someone who wasn't in the room when the image was made, and it quietly changes what the image says.

A crop is an opinion. It decides what the subject is, where the eye lands first, and what gets left outside as context the viewer will never know about. Treating it as a technical step — fit to frame, export, move on — gives that opinion away.

Three questions before the scissors

  • What is this image about? Not what's in it — what it's about. A portrait can be about a face or about a room.
  • What does the edge do? An edge that cuts through a hand creates tension. An edge with air around it creates calm. Neither is wrong; both are choices.
  • What will sit next to it? Crops talk to each other. Two tight crops side by side shout; a tight crop next to a loose one breathes.

Crop early, not last

The habit worth building is cropping while designing, not after. When the frame is part of the composition from the first sketch, the layout and the image can push against each other — and that friction is usually where the interesting page comes from.

If the crop could be done by an algorithm, it probably was.

None of this needs to be precious. It just needs to be decided.
